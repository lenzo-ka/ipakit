# Computational historical linguistics: the metric and the data (issue #105)

> Assessment, 2026-08-26. The metric-and-data half of computational historical linguistics; the rule-engine half is settled in [conlang.md](conlang.md). Q1 (sound classes) is **ruled by kal**: `ipa.xml` is a configuration, so a declared, overridable sound-class table is supportable, overturning [interop.md](interop.md) §5's refusal. Q2 (cognate alignment) and Q3 (reconstruction / two-level) are this assessment's verdicts, reached on re-derived evidence.

Issue #105 asks three questions about whether ipakit should reach into computational historical linguistics: sound classes, cognate alignment, and reconstruction. The rule-engine half of the same territory is already settled in [conlang.md](conlang.md) — relative chronology, feeding/bleeding/counterfeeding/counterbleeding all state and trace, and propagation-as-fixpoint is refused with a measurement. #105 is the **metric and data** half: not the rules, but the distance, the sound-class reductions those distances feed, and the inverse problem the whole field points at. kal reacted to each of the three questions, and the tiergraph seat sent a correction on the third; both are folded in and attributed where they land.

**Overall: ONE clear recommendation, ONE reframed support, ONE principled boundary.** Cognate *alignment* is the most promising and least-explored fit and is worth recommending on its substance (Q2). Sound classes are *supportable* — not as hardcoding to refuse, but as another declared, overridable configuration layer, once kal's reframe is taken (Q1). Reconstruction is bounded by a testable criterion rather than a flat refusal: **bidirectionality must not be bought by discarding the derivation trace** (Q3).

## Q1 — Sound classes are supportable, and the derivability number sizes the supplement

**kal's reframe changes the verdict, so state the new one first.** interop.md §5 refused sound classes on two grounds: deriving them from the features is measured impossible, and "a shipped sound-class table would be the hand-maintained second copy of the inventory `test_declared_not_hardcoded.py` exists to prevent" — §5's own wording for the objection. kal's reframe removes the second ground: `ipa.xml` is itself *a* configuration — usable, overridable, replaceable — so a sound-class system ships as **another declared, overridable configuration layer**, a supplement, on the same footing `ipa.xml` already has. A declared, overridable table is not a hidden second copy of the inventory; it is an explicit configuration whose contents a user can inspect, override or replace. So the verdict is not "refuse (violates declared-not-hardcoded)"; it is **supportable as a declared sound-class configuration**, and the derivability number no longer decides yes/no — it only says how much of the table falls out of the phonetic features for free and how much the configuration must state in its own right.

**Re-derived, and the prior figure is confirmed with its mechanism identified.** No CLTS clone is mounted on this machine (`IPAKIT_CLTS_DIR` unset; no `sounds.tsv` under `~/dev/lenzo`), so `scripts/interop.py classes` — which needs the clone through `pyclts` — cannot run here. `pyclts` 4.0.2 *is* installed, but its `api.soundclass()` reads the clone. I therefore re-derived directly from `ipakit-refs/papers/clts-2026-soundclasses-lingpy.tsv` (CLTS `pkg/soundclasses/lingpy.tsv`, commit `4da03b1`), which *is* LingPy's class system, using ipakit's own `natural_class` and `phones_matching` — the same two calls `cmd_classes` uses. A class is derivable when the features its members share re-extend under `phones_matching` to exactly its members. Method: for each of ipakit's 139 registered phones, take the class the tsv assigns its BIPA grapheme; 108 of 139 have a direct tsv row.

```
re-derived directly from lingpy.tsv (108 of 139 phones with a direct BIPA-grapheme row):
  sca     23 classes over 108 phones;  derivable: 4 of 22
  dolgo   12 classes over 108 phones;  derivable: 1 of 12
  asjp    39 classes over 108 phones;  derivable: 8 of 30
  cv       3 classes over 108 phones;  derivable: 1 of 3

interop.md §5, via pyclts over the full clone (139 of 139 phones):
  sca 4 of 22   dolgo 2 of 12   asjp 9 of 35   cv 2 of 3
```

**SCA reproduces exactly (4 of 22). The small deltas on the other three are a pure coverage artifact, and it is demonstrable rather than assumed.** The 31 phones with no direct tsv row are the affricates, diphthongs, `ä`, the rhotic vowels `ɚ ɝ`, `ɡ`, `ɫ` and the space marker — a set `pyclts` resolves by base-sound fallback and a raw grapheme lookup does not. Every class that differs from the doc is a vowel or near-vowel class truncated by those missing rows. Worked, for the one DOLGO class that flips: DOLGO's vowel class `V` has 28 direct members, and `natural_class` of those 28 already extends under `phones_matching` to exactly **37** phones — the 9 it adds (`a͜ɪ a͜ʊ e͜ə e͜ɪ o͜ʊ ä ɔ͜ɪ ɪ͜ə ʊ͜ə`) are precisely the diphthongs and `ä` absent from the tsv. So under full resolution `V`'s membership equals its natural-class extension and the class is exact, recovering the doc's second DOLGO derivable. The prior "2 of 12 DOLGO derivable" is **confirmed** under full resolution; my clone-free method gives 1 of 12 only because the vowel class is 28/37-complete. The exact identities of the doc's two derivables ("the vowels and one epiglottal pair") I take as its figure — I could not re-verify member-by-member without the clone, and I flag that rather than assert it.

**The qualitative finding is unchanged and, under partial coverage, sharper: the overwhelming majority of sound classes are not ipakit natural classes.** 18 of 22 SCA classes, 10–11 of 12 DOLGO, and most of ASJP do not re-extend. The ones that fail are the interesting ones and interop.md §5 already named why: DOLGO's `K` groups velars, uvulars, palatals, clicks *and* the alveolar affricates; `T` groups dentals, alveolars, retroflex stops *and* the postalveolar affricates. Those are not synchronic feature classes — they group segments by diachronic stability, a historical judgment, not a fact about a bundle. That is exactly the content a sound-class *configuration* would declare in its own right, the way CLTS's `lingpy.tsv` already does.

**So the crisp statement of the Q1 verdict:** ship a sound-class layer as a declared configuration (a table on the footing of `ipa.xml` — overridable, replaceable), and let the derivability measurement do one job only — mark which classes the configuration inherits from the phonetic features (≈ 4/22 SCA, 1–2/12 DOLGO) and which it must state itself (the diachronic groupings, the majority). A build could even ship the inheritable fraction *derived* and check it against the declared table, the way `xsampa.xml` is staleness-checked, so the configuration carries only the part features cannot supply. **RULED (kal, 2026-08-26): the reframe overturns interop.md §5's refusal.** `ipa.xml` is *a* configuration — usable, overridable, replaceable — so a declared sound-class table is configuration on that footing, not a hidden second copy of the inventory, and §5's refusal (which read a hand-maintained table as the thing `test_declared_not_hardcoded.py` exists to prevent) no longer holds. And the demand the measurement alone could not find is now named, and it is broader than sound classes: **freeze panphon's geometry at dev time and use its structures to compare ipakit's feature representations and processing against it.** That is the general case — a second declared configuration (another feature system) hosted alongside ipakit's own so the two are compared on the same evidence, which is the theory-neutral-substrate position stated concretely. panphon stays a dev-only dependency; freezing its geometry at dev time follows the pattern ipakit already uses for external data it must not depend on at runtime (the CLTS tables in `ipakit-refs/`, `xsampa.xml`'s checked derivation). A sound-class layer is one instance of that mechanism; the panphon-geometry comparison is the instance with a live use case. **Verdict: SUPPORTABLE as a declared, overridable configuration — RULED by kal; the derivability number sizes the supplement, it does not refuse it.**

## Q2 — Cognate alignment: the most promising fit, and it is concrete

**kal: "that sounds like a great application." It is, and the reason is that ipakit already has the two things LingPy's aligner is built out of.** LingPy's alignment is List's SCA method — Sound-Class-based Phonetic Alignment — and the `lingpy.tsv` of Q1 is literally its class system. Reading the primary (List 2012), SCA is three parts, and ipakit maps onto each:

1. **A scoring function over segment pairs, driving a dynamic-programming alignment matrix.** List 2012 is explicit that biology derives these empirically but "in linguistics it is common to derive scoring functions from phonetic features" (2012: §3.2, p. 6). ipakit's `distance` *is* a feature-derived scorer, and a strictly finer one: interop.md §0 re-derives **3,025 distinct ipakit distances against CLTS's 23** over the same 9,316 phone pairs. Where SCA reduces a sequence to a dozen sound classes before scoring, ipakit scores the segments directly on a graded metric. ipakit's metric is a drop-in for, and finer than, the scoring step SCA plugs in.
2. **Prosodic profiles: a per-segment weight derived from sonority.** SCA "assigns a specific score to each segment depending on its sonority," from a sonority hierarchy, giving seven prosodic environments — word-initial consonant, word-initial vowel, ascending sonority, sonority peak, descending sonority, word-final consonant, word-final vowel (List 2012: §3.4, pp. 12–13). ipakit already derives a sonority scale from its declarations: interop.md §5 re-derives it at **ρ = 0.938 against PanPhon's** over 115 shared phones, and `ipakit/syllable.py` carries the sonority ordering the library uses for syllabification. That is precisely the input SCA's prosodic profiles need, and ipakit derives it rather than tabulating it.
3. **Pairwise then multiple alignment: DPA for pairs, progressive alignment on a guide tree for sets** (List 2012: §3.3, pp. 10–11). ipakit ships the pairwise half today: `word_distance` returns a `WordDistanceResult` carrying an `Alignment` of `AlignmentStep`s, and `directional_word_distance`, `pairwise_distances`, `explain_word_distance` are alongside it.

**So what ipakit already has:** a graded, feature-derived segment metric (finer than the sound-class scorer SCA defaults to), a derived sonority scale (the prosodic-profile input), and pairwise phonetic alignment with a scored trace. **What it would need:** multiple-sequence alignment (the progressive/guide-tree layer over three or more forms), and — for cognate *detection* rather than alignment — LexStat's language-specific scorer, which List derives by permuting the wordlist to get language-pair-specific log-odds. That second thing is a wordlist computation, and it lands exactly on the line conlang.md §12 draws: "the line is a lexicon, and ipakit does not have one." `word_distance` takes two forms and answers about two forms; a cognate-detection run takes a wordlist with glosses and clusters it.

**The recommendation, stated to keep the two apart.** Cognate *alignment* — scoring and aligning forms with ipakit's metric, pairwise now and multiple with a guide-tree layer — is a strong, recommendable fit, because ipakit supplies a better scorer and the sonority input SCA already wants, and because alignment is a claim about forms, not a wordlist product. Cognate *detection / LexStat / clustering* is the wordlist product conlang.md §12 fences off as a second product with its own entry point. The honest pitch is the first: ipakit's distance can drive LingPy-style alignment, and does so with a finer scoring function than SCA's sound classes and a sonority profile it derives rather than declares. **Verdict: RECOMMEND cognate alignment (pairwise shipped, multiple-alignment the buildable increment); the metric is the differentiator. Detection is the wordlist line.**

## Q3 — Reconstruction and two-level: a boundary as a testable criterion, not a refusal

**kal: "this would be interesting to explore too." Reached on evidence, the answer is more interesting than a category error, and the tiergraph seat's correction sharpens it further.** The issue body called reconstruction-as-inverse "probably a category error"; the reframing comment, and now the seat's follow-up, replace that with a boundary that a next candidate formalism can be *tested* against.

**Rewrite-rules do not run backward, and that part is re-derived, not quoted.** calculus.md's own algebra says a rule set is "a *function from a form to a set*, not an arbitrary form-to-form relation. It cannot be inverted, intersected with another rule set, or complemented," resting it on Kaplan & Kay: "the regular relations differ from the regular languages … in that they are not closed under intersection and complementation" (calculus.md, re-read at HEAD `a8e7d07`; K&K 1994: 342). Invertibility in ipakit is *classified*, not offered — `Rule.invertibility(phoneset)` tests length preservation then injectivity, and reports escapes and absorptions per rule. So the rewrite branch genuinely does not invert.

**Constraint two-level grammars do run backward, by construction, and the primary says exactly how.** Reading Kaplan & Kay §7 in the primary: Koskenniemi (1983) "replaced the serial feeding arrangement of the independent generalizations in a rewriting grammar with a parallel method of combination. In eliminating the intermediate strings that pass from one rewriting rule to another, he also reduced to just two the number of linguistically meaningful levels of representation" (K&K 1994: 366–67). A pair of strings is accepted iff every constraint transducer accepts it simultaneously; there is no direction and no intermediate stage. The Karttunen–Beesley festschrift states the same history from Koskenniemi's side: the rules "would not be applied sequentially but in parallel. Each rule would constrain a certain lexical/surface correspondence and the environment in which the correspondence was allowed, required or prohibited" (Karttunen & Beesley 2005: 3, "A Short History of Two-Level Morphology"). And the two branches are equivalent in power: "Ordered rewriting grammars with boundaries and two-level constraint grammars with boundaries are equivalent in their expressive power" (K&K 1994: 367, Theorem) — so the choice between them "cannot be used to choose between them" on empirical coverage; it is a design choice about what representation you keep.

**What two-level costs is the trace, by design.** A two-level derivation "is not a sequence of forms but one correspondence that holds or does not, so there is nothing to trace — by design, not by omission" (calculus.md at HEAD; this is the #104 coupling below). That is the cost that matters to ipakit's audiences: there is nothing to show a student walking through an opaque derivation, and nothing for a g2p debugger to read when a front end mispronounces a word — which conlang.md §11 and interop.md §11 both identify as the thing this engine is *for* (`ã` is explained only by the intermediate `ãn`).

**The tiergraph seat's correction — folded in and attributed.** kal's original framing ("two-level has no intermediates, so bidirectionality is out of substrate here") smuggled a false premise: that discarding intermediates is the *only* way to buy bidirectionality. It is not. Two-level buys invertibility by having **no derivational stages** — nothing to invert. A **synchronous grammar** buys it a different way: the derivation is **shared** between the two sides, so one tree licenses both directions and every intermediate node survives; the price is paid in *expressiveness* (the two sides must be derivationally parallel), not in trace. So the principled boundary should be written as a **testable criterion, not a verdict on one family** (tiergraph seat):

> **Bidirectionality must not be bought by discarding the derivation trace.**

That criterion rules out constraint two-level (which buys bidirectionality by eliminating the intermediate strings — the trace *is* what it discards) *and* admits synchronous grammars (which keep every intermediate node because both directions read one shared derivation), and it hands the next candidate formalism a property to be tested against rather than a closed door. This is a refusal with a reason in the house form — the reason is the trace, and the reason generalizes to a criterion.

**The #104 coupling, re-derived rather than quoted.** #104 adds the finite-state citation to `docs/calculus.md` and names two-level as the contrast branch; re-reading calculus.md at HEAD `a8e7d07` confirms it does exactly that — it quotes K&K on Koskenniemi eliminating "the intermediate strings that pass from one rewriting rule to another" and states the equivalence theorem (367), then concludes "This side was taken because a phonology class and a g2p failure both need the step and not only the answer." The Q3 boundary here is the same fact stated as a criterion, and it should stay consistent with calculus.md's wording as #104 lands: two-level is the contrast branch precisely because it trades the trace for bidirectionality, and ipakit took the branch that keeps the trace. **Verdict: reconstruction-as-inverse is BOUNDED by a testable criterion — bidirectionality must not cost the trace. Two-level is out for that reason; synchronous grammars are in.**

## Implications for tiergraph

Observations for the sibling seat to weigh, flagged as tiergraph's call, not ipakit changes. The tiergraph checkout is stale and another seat's domain, so these are stated from #105's evidence and the seat's own input, not from reading that repo.

**1. The derivation-trace-vs-constraint tension is what tiergraph's tiered derivation graph is *for*.** Q3's criterion — bidirectionality without discarding the trace — is precisely the property a synchronous, tiered derivation graph can satisfy where a two-level constraint grammar cannot: the shared derivation keeps every intermediate node. The seat reports the tiergraph state honestly: recognize/count/best ship (0.1.0), *generate* is the open half — **the generate direction is not built**. (Two related facts must keep their subject: an unshipped parse-then-generate round trip, and a `reversible` flag no executor honors, are properties of **HSG** specifically, not tiergraph — as is the **lowering fidelity** `lift(lower(d)) == d` that HSG *does* ship, which that unshipped round trip should not be conflated with.) So the criterion is not yet met in tiergraph code even though the architecture is the right shape for it — that is a build target, not a settled capability.

**2. Sound-class-as-configuration (Q1) is a second concrete instance of the already-ruled theory-neutral-substrate / profiles position.** The generalization: commitment lives in a *profile*, and competing theories become competing profiles scored on the same evidence — feature systems and sound-class systems are two profiles hosted by one substrate, exactly as `ipa.xml`-as-overridable-config generalizes. The honest caveat (seat): profiles there are today a **convention with no mechanism** — no base type, no registry, no enumerability, no satisfaction check. So this is really a vote for building that mechanism first, and kept **elementary**: a registry plus a base type, not a categorical construction. Q1 gives it a second worked instance (a declared, overridable sound-class table beside the feature declarations); it does not itself supply the missing mechanism.

**3. Cognate alignment (Q2) and the seat's forced alignment want the same missing primitive.** Form-to-form alignment (Q2, aligning two transcriptions) and the seat's forced alignment (form-to-signal) are different subjects, but they want one thing in common: a **declared alignment relation** that is neither containment nor dependency. If that primitive lands, both are consumers — which is a stronger argument for building it than either subject alone. ipakit's `word_distance`/`Alignment` is the form-to-form consumer today; a declared alignment relation in the substrate would let the same relation carry both.
